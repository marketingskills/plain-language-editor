# AI-slop pattern reference

Adapted from FeedHive/ai-slop-detector (MIT). See patterns/SOURCE.md for the pinned source and attribution.

This is an editorial checklist, not an AI-authorship detector or a list of automatic bans.
A single pattern may be appropriate. Focus on repeated, distracting patterns and preserve meaning, evidence and the author's voice.
Do not invent examples, numbers or evidence just to fix a pattern. Treat suggestions as examples, not replacements to copy verbatim.

120 patterns across 9 categories.

## Vocabulary

Words that show up far more often in AI output than in human writing. One is a coincidence; three in a paragraph is a pattern.

### V-01 Delve and friends

delve, dive into, deep dive, unpack, explore (for "discuss").

**Example to check:** "Let's delve into the data."

**Example improvement:** "Here is what the data shows."

### V-02 Tapestry words

tapestry, landscape, realm, sphere, ecosystem, fabric (used abstractly).

**Example to check:** "the ever-evolving landscape of marketing."

**Example improvement:** "marketing in 2026" or name the actual change.

### V-03 Testament and beacon

testament to, beacon of, embodiment of, hallmark of.

**Example to check:** "The launch is a testament to the team's dedication."

**Example improvement:** "The team shipped it in six weeks."

### V-04 Pivotal and crucial

pivotal, crucial, vital, paramount, critical (as decoration).

**Example to check:** "plays a pivotal role in growth."

**Example improvement:** "drives 40% of signups."

### V-05 Showcase verbs

showcase, highlight, underscore, emphasize, spotlight (as commentary verbs).

**Example to check:** "The results underscore the importance of testing."

**Example improvement:** "Testing caught three bugs before launch."

### V-06 Foster and cultivate

foster, cultivate, nurture (applied to abstract things).

**Example to check:** "fostering a culture of innovation."

**Example improvement:** Say what the team actually does.

### V-07 Leverage and utilize

leverage, utilize, harness, capitalize on.

**Example to check:** "Leverage AI to streamline your workflow."

**Example improvement:** "Use AI to draft the weekly report."

### V-08 Elevate and enhance

elevate, enhance, empower, supercharge, unlock, amplify.

**Example to check:** "Elevate your content strategy."

**Example improvement:** Name the result: "Post three times a week without writing on weekends."

### V-09 Seamless and robust

seamless, robust, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, best-in-class, world-class.

**Example to check:** "a seamless, robust solution."

**Example improvement:** "it syncs in under a second and hasn't gone down this year."

### V-10 Intricate and multifaceted

intricate, multifaceted, nuanced, complex (as filler).

**Example to check:** "the intricate interplay of factors."

**Example improvement:** Name two of the factors.

### V-11 Meticulous

meticulous, meticulously, painstakingly, carefully crafted.

**Example to check:** "meticulously crafted templates."

**Example improvement:** "templates tested on 200 posts."

### V-12 Vibrant and rich

vibrant, rich, bustling, thriving, dynamic.

**Example to check:** "a vibrant community of creators."

**Example improvement:** "4,000 creators who post weekly."

### V-13 Journey and embark

journey, embark, navigate, voyage, path (for ordinary processes).

**Example to check:** "embark on your SEO journey."

**Example improvement:** "start with your top ten pages."

### V-14 Game changer

game changer, revolutionary, groundbreaking, transformative, paradigm shift.

**Example to check:** "This feature is a game changer."

**Example improvement:** Say what changes and for whom.

### V-15 Transition adverbs

Additionally, Furthermore, Moreover, Notably, Importantly (opening sentences).

**Example to check:** "Additionally, the tool supports exports."

**Example improvement:** "It also exports to CSV."

### V-16 Empty intensifiers

truly, incredibly, deeply, profoundly, remarkably, fundamentally.

**Example to check:** "a truly remarkable result."

**Example improvement:** Give the number that makes it remarkable.

### V-17 Quietly

quietly, silently, subtly (adding drama to plain events).

**Example to check:** "Costs quietly crept up."

**Example improvement:** "Costs rose 12% in March."

### V-18 Align and resonate

align with, resonate with, speak to, in line with.

**Example to check:** "content that resonates with your audience."

**Example improvement:** "posts your audience replies to."

### V-19 Key as an adjective

key insight, key takeaway, key factor, key role.

**Example to check:** "The key takeaway is consistency."

**Example improvement:** "Post on the same days every week."

### V-20 Era words

in today's fast-paced world, in the digital age, in an era of, now more than ever.

**Example to check:** "In today's fast-paced digital world, content is king."

**Example improvement:** Cut it and start with the point.

## Stock phrases

Ready-made phrases that fill space without adding meaning.

### P-01 Worth noting

it's worth noting, it's important to note, it should be noted.

**Example to check:** "It's worth noting that the API is rate limited."

**Example improvement:** "The API allows 100 calls a minute."

### P-02 At its core

at its core, at the end of the day, when all is said and done, ultimately.

**Example to check:** "At its core, marketing is about people."

**Example improvement:** Delete, or say something specific about people.

### P-03 When it comes to

when it comes to, in terms of, with regard to, in the world of.

**Example to check:** "When it comes to scheduling, timing matters."

**Example improvement:** "Posts at 9am get more reach than posts at 3pm."

### P-04 Let's dive in

let's dive in, let's get started, buckle up, without further ado.

**Example to check:** "Ready? Let's dive in!"

**Example improvement:** Start with the first point.

### P-05 Plays a role

plays a role in, contributes to, is a factor in.

**Example to check:** "Hashtags play a role in reach."

**Example improvement:** "Posts with 3 to 5 hashtags reached more people in our test."

### P-06 Stands as

stands as, serves as, functions as, acts as (instead of "is").

**Example to check:** "The dashboard serves as a central hub."

**Example improvement:** "The dashboard shows every scheduled post."

### P-07 Navigate the complexities

navigate the complexities of, the ins and outs of, the nuances of.

**Example to check:** "helping you navigate the complexities of SEO."

**Example improvement:** "explains how Google picks which page to rank."

### P-08 Whether you're X or Y

Whether you're a beginner or a pro, from startups to enterprises.

**Example to check:** "Whether you're a solo creator or a global brand, ..."

**Example improvement:** Say who it is actually for.

### P-09 Look no further

look no further, you've come to the right place, this is your ultimate guide.

**Example to check:** "Looking for a scheduler? Look no further."

**Example improvement:** "FeedHive schedules posts to 10 platforms."

### P-10 Unlock the power

unlock the power of, harness the potential of, take it to the next level.

**Example to check:** "Unlock the full potential of your content."

**Example improvement:** Say the specific gain.

### P-11 Here's the thing

here's the thing, here's the deal, let me be clear, I'll be honest, I'll be straight with you.

**Example to check:** "Here's the thing: most people quit."

**Example improvement:** "Most people quit in month two."

### P-12 The truth is

the truth is, the reality is, the uncomfortable truth, the hard truth.

**Example to check:** "The hard truth is that SEO takes time."

**Example improvement:** "SEO takes about six months to show results."

### P-13 Sign-off clichés

I hope this helps, happy writing, feel free to reach out, let me know if you have any questions.

**Example to check:** A post or article ending with "Hope this helps!"

**Example improvement:** End on the last useful point.

### P-14 Summary openers

In conclusion, In summary, To sum up, Overall, All in all.

**Example to check:** "In conclusion, consistency is key."

**Example improvement:** End on the last concrete point; the reader was just there.

### P-15 Vague future

the future is bright, the possibilities are endless, only time will tell, the sky's the limit.

**Example to check:** "With AI, the possibilities are endless."

**Example improvement:** Name one thing that is now possible.

## Sentence patterns

Constructions that AI reaches for again and again.

### S-01 Not X, but Y

"It's not X. It's Y." / "This isn't about X, it's about Y."

**Example to check:** "It's not a tool. It's a teammate."

**Example improvement:** Say what it does: "It drafts replies for you to approve."

### S-02 Not just X, but also Y

"It's not just fast, it's also accurate."

**Example to check:** "Not just a scheduler, but a full content engine."

**Example improvement:** List what it does.

### S-03 The question isn't X

"The question isn't whether, it's when." / "The problem isn't X. The problem is Y."

**Example to check:** "The question isn't if you should use AI. It's how."

**Example improvement:** "Here is how we use AI for first drafts."

### S-04 Negative listing

"Not a X. Not a Y. A Z."

**Example to check:** "Not a plan. Not a hope. A system."

**Example improvement:** "A system: three posts a week, written on Mondays."

### S-05 Rhetorical Q and A

A question the writer immediately answers.

**Example to check:** "Why does this matter? Because reach is everything."

**Example improvement:** "Reach decides how many people see the post."

### S-06 The obvious question

"The obvious question is...", "You might be wondering..."

**Example to check:** "You might be wondering how this works."

**Example improvement:** Explain how it works.

### S-07 Colon reveal

A short setup, a colon, then a dramatic reveal.

**Example to check:** "The best part: it's free."

**Example improvement:** "It's free."

### S-08 Faux insight

"What nobody tells you", "the part most people miss", "here's what most get wrong".

**Example to check:** "What nobody tells you about growth: distribution beats product."

**Example improvement:** "Distribution brought us more users than any feature."

### S-09 This is where it gets interesting

this is where it gets interesting, this changes everything, and that's where things get wild.

**Example to check:** "And this is where it gets interesting."

**Example improvement:** State the interesting thing.

### S-10 The useful part is

the useful part, the part where, the best part, the exciting part, the part that stuck with me.

**Example to check:** "The useful part is that it connects to your inbox."

**Example improvement:** "It connects to your inbox."

### S-11 X is just Y

Calling something "just" a vivid unrelated thing to make a point.

**Example to check:** "Posting without a plan is just gambling with your time."

**Example improvement:** "Without a plan you post less and repeat topics."

### S-12 Trailing -ing analysis

A sentence ends with an -ing clause that tells you what it means.

**Example to check:** "Sales rose 20%, highlighting the strength of the brand."

**Example improvement:** "Sales rose 20%."

### S-13 Rule of three

Lists of exactly three adjectives or phrases, by reflex.

**Example to check:** "fast, simple, and powerful."

**Example improvement:** Keep the one that is true and provable.

### S-14 Synonym cycling

Rotating names for the same thing to avoid repetition.

**Example to check:** "The agent drafts. The assistant reviews. The tool publishes."

**Example improvement:** "The agent drafts, reviews and publishes."

### S-15 Arbitrary "one"

Putting "one" before a noun to sound focused when the number means nothing.

**Example to check:** "Start with one workflow."

**Example improvement:** "Start with your weekly newsletter."

### S-16 Fake-strong verbs

boasts, features, offers, maintains (instead of "has").

**Example to check:** "The app boasts an intuitive interface."

**Example improvement:** "The app has a drag-and-drop calendar."

### S-17 Imagine a world

imagine a world where, picture this, what if I told you.

**Example to check:** "Imagine a world where your posts write themselves."

**Example improvement:** Describe what the product does today.

### S-18 Hedge stacking

may potentially, could possibly, might help to, can sometimes.

**Example to check:** "This may potentially help improve results."

**Example improvement:** "This improved reply rate by 8% in our test." Or cut it.

### S-19 Both-sides filler

"While X has benefits, it also has drawbacks."

**Example to check:** "While automation saves time, it's important to stay authentic."

**Example improvement:** Take a position and support it.

### S-20 From X to Y range

"From startups to enterprises", "from strategy to execution".

**Example to check:** "From ideation to publication, we've got you covered."

**Example improvement:** Name the two steps you actually handle.

### S-21 Interpretive asides

Telling the reader how to read the text.

**Example to check:** "That last point matters more than it sounds."

**Example improvement:** Show why it matters, or delete the aside.

### S-22 Rhetorical self-correction

"Actually, let me rephrase that", "Or rather..."

**Example to check:** "It's fast. Or rather, it's instant."

**Example improvement:** "It's instant."

## Structure and rhythm

Slop is often in the shape of the text, not the words.

### R-01 One-line paragraph stacks

Many short lines in a row, each a slogan.

**Example to check:** "I quit my job. / I built a product. / Nobody came. / Then I learned this."

**Example improvement:** Write it as one paragraph.

### R-02 Fragment drumbeat

Strings of fragments for rhythm.

**Example to check:** "Code. Content. Customers. Every day."

**Example improvement:** One full sentence that says what you do every day.

### R-03 Repeated starters

Several lines that start with the same word or phrase.

**Example to check:** "It saves time. It saves money. It saves your sanity."

**Example improvement:** "It saves about four hours a week."

### R-04 Identical sentence shapes

Every sentence the same length and structure.

**Example to check:** A paragraph of 12-word sentences, each "Subject verb object, comma, clause."

**Example improvement:** Vary length when it helps the point.

### R-05 List dumps

Long inventories of nouns in one breath.

**Example to check:** "sales, marketing, support, ops, finance, hiring, legal and product."

**Example improvement:** Pick the two that matter here.

### R-06 Bullets for everything

Bullet points where two sentences of prose would read better.

**Example to check:** Three bullets of four words each.

**Example improvement:** One sentence.

### R-07 Headings on tiny sections

Headers over one or two sentences.

**Example to check:** "## Why it matters" followed by one line.

**Example improvement:** Fold it into the paragraph above.

### R-08 Recap ending

A final paragraph that repeats the whole piece.

**Example to check:** "To recap, we covered A, B and C."

**Example improvement:** End on the last point or the next action.

### R-09 Mic-drop ending

A final "deep" one-liner.

**Example to check:** "The future isn't coming. It's already here."

**Example improvement:** End on the clearest concrete sentence.

### R-10 Challenges and future outlook

A formulaic "Despite challenges, the future looks promising" closer.

**Example to check:** "Despite these challenges, the outlook remains positive."

**Example improvement:** Name the challenge and what is being done.

### R-11 Fake-unscripted scaffolding

"And honestly...", "And look...", "So yeah..." used as filler.

**Example to check:** "And honestly? That's when it clicked."

**Example improvement:** Say what clicked.

### R-12 Over-structured short answers

A short reply split into sections with headers and bold labels.

**Example to check:** A three-sentence answer with "Overview", "Key Points" and "Conclusion".

**Example improvement:** Three sentences.

## Imagery and metaphor

AI likes making things vivid when they should be clear.

### M-01 Cute metaphors

Abstract things doing human or physical actions.

**Example to check:** "Your data is whispering insights."

**Example improvement:** "Your data shows Tuesday posts get more replies."

### M-02 Things that "live" or "sit"

Data, products or agents that live or sit somewhere.

**Example to check:** "where your customer data lives."

**Example improvement:** "where your customer data is stored."

### M-03 Honest dashboards

Systems that "get honest", "tell the truth", "hide" or "reveal".

**Example to check:** "Analytics finally get honest."

**Example improvement:** "Analytics now count only real views."

### M-04 Mixed and stacked metaphors

Two or three images in one sentence.

**Example to check:** "a north star that fuels the engine of growth."

**Example improvement:** Drop both images and say what drives growth.

### M-05 Fuel and engine

fuel, engine, rocket, catalyst, springboard, flywheel (as decoration).

**Example to check:** "content that fuels your growth engine."

**Example improvement:** "posts that bring in signups."

### M-06 Secret sauce

secret sauce, magic, silver bullet, holy grail.

**Example to check:** "Consistency is the secret sauce."

**Example improvement:** "Accounts that posted weekly grew twice as fast."

## Substance

The deepest kind of slop: text that sounds informative and says nothing.

### C-01 Importance puffery

Inflating significance: "marks a pivotal moment", "a significant shift".

**Example to check:** "This marks a pivotal moment for the industry."

**Example improvement:** State the fact and let the reader judge.

### C-02 Vague attribution

experts agree, studies show, many believe, it is widely regarded.

**Example to check:** "Studies show posting daily works."

**Example improvement:** Name the study, or cut the claim.

### C-03 Generic claims without receipts

Claims any company could make.

**Example to check:** "AI can transform your business."

**Example improvement:** "Our AI drafts a week of posts from a 3-line brief."

### C-04 The portability test

A sentence that could move unchanged to another company or topic.

**Example to check:** "We're committed to delivering value to our customers."

**Example improvement:** A sentence only you could write.

### C-05 Fake-profound wisdom

Lines that sound deep and mean nothing concrete.

**Example to check:** "Growth is a mindset, not a metric."

**Example improvement:** Say what you measure and why.

### C-06 Smoothing the specific

Replacing a sharp fact with a generic description.

**Example to check:** "a leading expert in the field."

**Example improvement:** "who wrote the Kubernetes scheduler."

### C-07 Made-up specifics

Invented numbers, names or examples presented as real.

**Example to check:** "Sarah, a marketing manager, saved 10 hours a week." (no Sarah exists)

**Example improvement:** A real example, or none.

### C-08 Telling instead of showing

Labeling something surprising, important or interesting.

**Example to check:** "Surprisingly, the simple version won."

**Example improvement:** "The simple version got twice the clicks."

### C-09 Canned notability

"featured in top outlets", "widely recognized", "has an active social media presence".

**Example to check:** "She has been featured in numerous leading publications."

**Example improvement:** Name one, with a link.

### C-10 Superficial balance

Every point gets a "however" so the text never commits.

**Example to check:** "AI is powerful. However, it has limitations."

**Example improvement:** Say which limitation matters for this reader.

### C-11 Advice with no steps

Telling the reader to "optimize", "be strategic" or "stay consistent" without saying how.

**Example to check:** "Optimize your posting schedule."

**Example improvement:** "Post Tuesday to Thursday between 8 and 10am."

### C-12 Hollow promises

"Everything you need to know", "the ultimate guide", "master X in minutes".

**Example to check:** "The ultimate guide to LinkedIn."

**Example improvement:** "How to write a LinkedIn post that gets replies."

## Formatting and punctuation



### F-01 Em dash habit

Em dashes used everywhere for rhythm.

**Example to check:** "It's fast — really fast — and free."

**Example improvement:** "It's fast and free." Use commas, periods or parentheses.

### F-02 Bold sprinkled mid-sentence

Random words in bold for emphasis.

**Example to check:** "This is **really important** for **growth**."

**Example improvement:** No bold; let the sentence carry it.

### F-03 Emoji as bullets

✅ 🚀 💡 👉 used as list markers or decoration.

**Example to check:** "✅ Faster ✅ Cheaper ✅ Better"

**Example improvement:** A plain sentence.

### F-04 Title Case Headings

Every Word Capitalized In Headings.

**Example to check:** "How To Grow Your Audience On LinkedIn"

**Example improvement:** "How to grow your audience on LinkedIn"

### F-05 Colon-label lines

Every line starts with a bold label and a colon.

**Example to check:** "**Speed:** fast. **Price:** low. **Support:** great."

**Example improvement:** A sentence about the one that matters.

### F-06 Markdown in the wrong place

Asterisks, hashtags or headers pasted into emails, posts or forms.

**Example to check:** "**Subject:** ..." inside a LinkedIn post.

**Example improvement:** Plain text.

### F-07 Curly-quote and character mix

Curly quotes, special dashes and non-breaking spaces mixed with plain text.

**Example to check:** “quotes” next to "quotes" in one paragraph.

**Example improvement:** Pick one style.

### F-08 Exclamation inflation

Exclamation marks on ordinary sentences.

**Example to check:** "We updated the settings page!"

**Example improvement:** "We updated the settings page."

### F-09 Unicode bold and fancy fonts in long posts

𝗕𝗼𝗹𝗱 or 𝓈𝒸𝓇𝒾𝓅𝓉 letters across whole lines.

**Example to check:** An entire LinkedIn post in Unicode bold.

**Example improvement:** Plain text, one bold phrase at most (screen readers can't read these).

### F-10 Tables for two facts

A table where a sentence would do.

**Example to check:** A 2x2 table comparing price and speed.

**Example improvement:** "A is cheaper; B is faster."

## Chatbot residue

Text that was clearly copied out of a chat window.

### B-01 Assistant openers

Certainly!, Absolutely!, Great question!, Of course!

**Example to check:** "Certainly! Here's a LinkedIn post about..."

**Example improvement:** Delete it.

### B-02 Sycophancy

You're absolutely right, what a great idea, I love this.

**Example to check:** An email reply starting "Great point, you're absolutely right!"

**Example improvement:** Answer the point.

### B-03 Offer to do more

Would you like me to..., Let me know if you'd like a shorter version.

**Example to check:** A published post ending "Would you like me to adjust the tone?"

**Example improvement:** Delete it.

### B-04 Here is a...

Lead-ins like "Here is a polished version of your post:" left in the final text.

**Example improvement:** Delete the line.

### B-05 Placeholder text

[Your Name], [Company], [insert statistic], XX%.

**Example improvement:** Fill it in or cut the sentence.

### B-06 AI disclaimers

As an AI, I don't have personal opinions; my knowledge is limited to...

**Example improvement:** Delete it.

### B-07 Source hedging

"Based on available information", "specific details are limited".

**Example to check:** "While specific figures are not widely available, ..."

**Example improvement:** Find the figure or don't mention it.

### B-08 Citation debris

Leftover markers like [1], contentReference, oaicite, turn0search0.

**Example improvement:** Remove them, and check the claim they were attached to.

## Social media and LinkedIn

Patterns that turn a post into "LinkedIn slop".

### L-01 Broetry

One sentence per line, top to bottom.

**Example to check:** A 20-line post where every line is a new sentence.

**Example improvement:** Short paragraphs.

### L-02 Hook formulas

"I was fired. Best thing that ever happened.", "Unpopular opinion:", "Stop doing X."

**Example improvement:** Open with the specific thing that happened or the specific claim.

### L-03 Thrilled to announce

I'm thrilled/excited/humbled to announce, I'm proud to share.

**Example to check:** "I'm thrilled to announce I've joined Acme!"

**Example improvement:** "I joined Acme to lead the growth team."

### L-04 Read that again

Read that again., Let that sink in., I'll say it louder.

**Example improvement:** Delete it. If the line needs a second read, rewrite it.

### L-05 Agree? / Thoughts?

Engagement-bait closers.

**Example to check:** "Agree? 👇"

**Example improvement:** Ask a real question, or don't.

### L-06 The lesson list

"Here are 7 lessons I learned from..." with generic lessons.

**Example improvement:** One lesson with the story behind it.

### L-07 Humblebrag setup

"I don't usually post this, but...", "This might be controversial, but..."

**Example improvement:** Post it without the preamble.

### L-08 Thread markers

🧵, 1/, "A thread" on content that isn't a thread.

**Example improvement:** Write the post.

### L-09 Hashtag walls

Ten or more hashtags at the end.

**Example improvement:** Three relevant ones, or none.

### L-10 That's it. That's the post

Self-aware closers that announce the ending.

**Example to check:** "That's it. That's the tweet."

**Example improvement:** Just stop.

### L-11 P.S. engagement

"P.S. Repost ♻️ if this helped." on every post.

**Example improvement:** Drop it, or use it rarely.

### L-12 No fluff, just value

Announcing quality instead of delivering it: no fluff, just value; straight to the point; real talk.

**Example improvement:** Delete the announcement.

### L-13 Comment slop

Generic replies: "Great insights!", "So true!", "Thanks for sharing, this is gold."

**Example improvement:** Reply to something specific in the post, or don't reply.

### L-14 Fake vulnerability arc

"I failed. I was broke. Then I discovered X." built to sell.

**Example improvement:** Tell what happened, with dates and numbers.

### L-15 Contrarian template

"Everyone says X. They're wrong."

**Example improvement:** Make your claim and back it up; skip the strawman.
